# Aprende y Planea — what the research says, and what changed

Date: 2026-11-18. Desk research only: published surveys, studies, statutes and court pages, each cited in the three full
reports below. **No client or staff has been interviewed yet**; the two instruments for that are drafted and wait for the
firm. Treat every "why people do X" claim as evidence about groups, not about any one client.

Full reports: [pain points](aprende/research/R1-painpoints.md) · [the process, from the client's side](aprende/research/R2-process.md) ·
[market and learning](aprende/research/R3-market-and-learning.md). Instruments: [staff questionnaire](aprende/research/staff-questionnaire.md) ·
[client interview guide](aprende/research/client-interview-guide.md) (draft; needs the firm's yes).

## What we learned

1. **The gap is real and not only about money.** 14% of Hispanic adults have a will vs 28% of White adults (Caring.com/YouGov
   2025, n≈2,500); 76% of Hispanic homeowners over 50 have no will or trust vs 35% of White homeowners (Urban Institute 2024).
   With wealth, homeownership and education held equal, Hispanic respondents are still about 18 points less likely to have
   a will (Boston College CRR 2023).
2. **People put it off; they don't refuse.** "Haven't gotten around to it" is the top reason in every survey (43% Caring 2025;
   61% of adults 50+, AARP 2024). Next: "I don't have enough", not knowing how to start, not wanting to think about death,
   cost. Among Hispanic adults 50+ without a will, 30% "don't want to think about it" (16% overall, AARP 2024).
3. **Setting matters more than money off.** In a survey experiment, a $500 incentive moved only White and financially
   sophisticated respondents; a calmer, simpler setting moved others (CRR 2023). The discount is a fair reward, not the reason
   people will come.
4. **Fear and trust.** About half of Latinos fear that they or someone close could be deported (Pew 2025; KFF/NYT 2025).
   "Notario" fraud is common in Los Angeles; State Bar non-attorney practice cases rose from 761 (FY20) to 1,223 (FY25).
5. **Plans fail after signing.** Trusts that are never funded, beneficiary forms that override the will, witnesses who
   inherit, and directives witnessed only by relatives are the common failures. No source measures how often trusts go
   unfunded.
6. **Teaching first improves understanding; it has not been shown to save attorney time.** A Cochrane review of 209 trials:
   knowledge +12 points out of 100, but consults were not shorter. Spanish, easy-to-read advance-planning tools raised
   completed directives in a trial (PREPARE: 33% → 43%).
7. **Our learning design is mostly on target.** Streak messages raise use (NBER 2025, n=60,000); a broken streak hurts less
   when it can be repaired and the person is not blamed (JCR 2023) — the "su vela descansó" rule. Retrieval practice is the
   best-supported part; game mechanics add a small effect.
8. **Competitors** cost $0–$499 (LegalZoom, Trust & Will, WillMaker, FreeWill), are not law firms, offer no Spanish
   documents, and draw most complaints for auto-renewing billing.

## What changed now

| Where | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lesson `trust.1.2` | "at least 9 months" → "9 to 18 months, sometimes longer", citing the courts' formal-probate page | the courts' own figure |
| Probate bonus lesson | New step: the simpler court order for a home worth $750,000 or less (§ 13151), the small-estate declaration (§ 13100), statutory fees on the **gross** value with a $500,000 example (§§ 10800, 10810), and "which route fits you is a question for the attorney" | the law changed in 2025; the old framing implied every home faces full probate |
| Will lesson | Opens with "a will is not only for people who have a lot"; the handwritten-will item now says an undated one can cause doubt (§ 6111(b)) | the top two reasons people give; the old item read as an equal alternative |
| Day-1 disclosure | Adds "I help you learn and gather your information. Attorney Raval reviews and decides everything." | disclose early, and make the human role visible |
| Verified statutes | §§ 10800, 10810, 13100, 13151 read on leginfo today and added; § 6111(b) added to its summary | every legal statement cites what we read |
| Attorney packet | A "changes after research" section lists every new or changed item for approval | nothing publishes without his approval |

## What changes in the plan

- **Character personas (T5-07):** frame around protecting the family and the house, not death; answer "I don't have enough"
  and "I trust my family" directly; never ask about immigration status (if the client raises it, it becomes a question for
  the attorney); never sell — payment stays on the firm's own screens; never pair a trust with insurance or annuities.
- **Intake questions (T5-05):** how the house is titled and who is on the deed (including a relative who has died); every
  account with a beneficiary form and who is named; any earlier will, trust or handwritten note and where it is; whether the
  client lives in or may move to a nursing facility (it changes who may witness the directive); family asked without
  assuming marriage (partner, children of another relationship, grandchildren being raised at home); guardian with an
  alternate and "not decided yet" allowed.
- **Conversation test set (T5-08):** add Spanglish, speech-to-text errors and repetition; a client afraid of immigration
  questions; "I don't have enough"; "is this a notario?".
- **After signing (Phase 6):** funding check-ins at 2, 6 and 12 weeks until staff mark funding done, then a yearly review;
  a signing checklist of who may witness each document.
- **Price card:** say what the $3,000 buys (written for you by a California attorney, reviewed before you see it, Spanish
  explanations, witnesses arranged, funding checklist); one price, never a subscription; no countdowns.
- **What we promise:** better-prepared clients and more finished plans. **Not** attorney time saved — the pilot measures it.

## What the pilot should measure

Lesson completion per day; share of clients reaching payment, signing and funding done; attorney minutes per matter vs the
firm's current average; change requests per matter; a one-question comprehension check ("¿para qué sirve este documento?")
at the start and end of each core lesson; five-person reading tests in Spanish on the will and health-care lessons.
