# Interview analysis sheet

For whoever reads the checked notes (Kevin, or firm staff). Use it after all 5–8 interviews. Input: the notes files
(`interview-notes-template.md`), never the signed forms or recordings.

## 1. Code book
Read each set of notes. Each time a passage fits a theme, mark the code next to it. One passage can have more than one code.
Also mark whether the passage is positive (+), negative (−) or mixed (±) when that matters (CHAR, PRICE, LEN).

| Code | Theme | Counts when the person… / what it covers | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|---|---|
| TRIG | Trigger to plan | What made the person think about a plan. | "When my mother got sick I started thinking about it." |
| DELAY | Delay reasons | Why they waited or stopped. | "I'll do it when I'm older." "We never agreed as a family." |
| TRUST | Trust and fraud fear | Doubts about lawyers, fear of being cheated. | "You never know who is honest." |
| NOTA | Notario experience | Any mention of notarios or non-lawyers offering wills, good or bad. | "A notario did my uncle's will for cheap." |
| COST | Cost expectations | What they think a will or trust costs, or what seems fair. | "A will is maybe $200." |
| FAM | Family conversation | Who they talk with, and how easy or hard it is. | "My kids don't want to hear about it." |
| KIDS | Children and guardian awareness | What they know about who cares for children if parents can't. | "The grandparents would take them, I guess." |
| PROB | Probate awareness | What they know or believe about court after a death. | "My cousin spent two years in court for a house." |
| SURP | Unexpected costs awareness | Q6 answers: house without court (TOD deed), property tax on an inherited home, Medi-Cal paying back. | "I never heard that Medi-Cal asks for money back." |
| CHAR | Reaction to characters | Feelings about La Calavera, El Oso, Piñata. | "The skull is funny, but my mother would not like it." |
| LEN | Lesson length | Reaction to 5 minutes a day for two weeks. | "Five minutes is fine, but not every day." |
| PRICE | Price-mechanic reaction | Reaction to the idea that the app may lower the price as they finish lessons. | "That makes me want to finish." "Sounds like a trick." |
| VOICE | Voice vs typing | Preference for talking or typing, and why. | "I'd rather talk; my spelling is bad." |
| PRIV | Privacy worries | Worry about giving address, property or family details. | "I don't want my information on the internet." |
| SCREEN | Screen confusion | A word, button or idea on a screen they did not understand. | "What does 'fideicomiso' mean here?" |

If something important does not fit, add a new code at the bottom and define it in one line.

## 3. When is a finding solid?
- **Solid:** the same point from **3 or more people**, from at least 2 of the 3 groups (a, b, c).
- **Worth watching:** 2 people, or 3+ from one group only.
- **Single voice:** 1 person. Report it only if it points to a risk (for example, a screen that misleads, or a fear of fraud).
- Check the subgroups: people over 60, people with children under 18, Spanish vs English.
- A strong quote does not make a finding solid by itself. Count people, not quotes.

## 4. Output: a one-page summary for Kevin and attorney Raval
1. **Who we talked to:** number of people by group, age range and language (no other details).
2. **Solid findings (3–5):** one line each, the count (for example "5 of 7"), and one short quote.
3. **Worth watching (up to 3).**
4. **Screens to fix:** each confusing word or screen and how many people it confused.
5. **Questions for the attorney:** the general questions people raised, merged and without duplicates.
6. **Changes we suggest for the app,** each tied to a finding.
