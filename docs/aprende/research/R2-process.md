# R2 — The real process of getting a California estate plan done, where clients stall, and what goes wrong

Prepared 2026-11-18 for the Aprende y Planea team (Carbon Law Group). Research only; no repository files were changed.

**How to read this.** Each finding names its source, URL and year. Survey findings give the sample size and who ran it.
"Evidence" means what a source says. "Inference" means my reasoning from that evidence, and it is labelled.
Statute text was read on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov on 2026-11-18 unless a line says otherwise. Two sections I cite
(Gov. Code 8211 and 8219.5) were read on FindLaw's copy ("current as of January 1, 2026"), not on leginfo.
Sources dated before 2019 are marked **[older]**.

## Summary

- **The small-estate limits changed on April 1, 2025, and one official page is out of date.** For deaths on or after
  April 1, 2025, the Probate Code 13100 affidavit limit is **$208,850**. A new court petition for a **primary residence
  worth up to $750,000** also applies (Probate Code 13151, AB 2016). The Judicial Council's form DE-300 (rev. April 28, 2025) and the
  self-help "simple transfer" page show these amounts. The self-help "small estate affidavit" page still says **$184,500**.
  Aprende's probate lesson gives no amounts today. Any amount it adds must be keyed to the date of death.
- **Formal probate takes 9 to 18 months, according to the California courts.** The statutory fees are a sliding
  percentage of the **gross** estate. Debts and mortgages are not subtracted (Probate Code 10800/10810(b)). On a $500,000
  estate the fees are $13,000 for the attorney plus $13,000 for the executor (my arithmetic from the statute).
  This is the honest "why" behind the trust lesson. But the 2025 home petition means some modest estates no longer
  need full probate even without a trust.
- **For the 50+ Latino client, the main barrier is emotional and practical, not legal.** In AARP's 2024
  probability survey (NORC panel, n=1,869; 389 Hispanic adults; interviews in English and Spanish), **35.7%** of Hispanic
  adults 50+ had a will. Among Hispanic adults without a will (n=250), **30.0%** said they "don't want to think about it"
  (16.4% of all respondents said this). **54.4%** said they "haven't gotten around to it". **6.7%** said they "don't trust the process"
  (1.8% overall).
- **The most common way an estate plan fails is that the client never finishes the steps after signing.** Trusts that
  were never funded go back to court (a Heggstad petition, or probate). The State Bar's Trusts & Estates Section has called
  Heggstad outcomes "very unpredictable" (2015 proposal). Beneficiary forms that were never updated also defeat the plan.
  In AARP's survey, only 62.1% of Hispanic adults 50+ with accounts had named a beneficiary on all of them, and 21.4% on none.
  I found **no primary data on how often trusts go unfunded**. Practitioner sources only describe it as common.
- **The signing rules differ by document, and the mistakes are predictable.** A will needs two witnesses present at the same time
  (6110). A beneficiary who witnesses creates a presumption of undue influence (6112). A POA needs a notary *or* two witnesses,
  and the agent cannot witness (4121–4122). An AHCD has its own witness exclusions, and at least one witness must be unrelated and not an heir (4674(e)). A skilled-nursing-facility patient needs an
  ombudsman witness (4675). An electronic AHCD must be notarized (4673(b)). A TOD deed needs two witnesses *and* a notary, and must be recorded
  within 60 days (5624, 5626).
- **Handwritten wills are legal but risky.** Probate Code 6111 makes a holographic will valid without witnesses. But a missing
  date can void it where it conflicts with another will, or where capacity is in doubt (6111(b)). Aprende's will lesson
  currently presents the handwritten will neutrally.
- **Notario fraud is well documented for immigration. I found no data specific to estate planning.** Government Code 8219.5
  bans the literal Spanish translation "notario público" in notary advertising. The State Bar's nonattorney
  unauthorized-practice caseload rose from 761 cases opened (FY2020) to 1,223 (FY2025), with no breakdown by practice area
  (2025 Annual Discipline Report). The California Attorney General separately warns about "living trust mills" that target seniors.
- **Two costs that often surprise families come from outside probate law.** Under Proposition 19, a child who inherits the
  family home keeps the parent's tax base only if the child moves in as a primary residence within one year (BOE fact sheet).
  For deaths on or after January 1, 2017, Medi-Cal estate recovery reaches only the *probate* estate (DHCS, SB 833). That makes
  trust funding or a TOD deed relevant for clients on Medi-Cal.

**Conflict in the evidence (registry fee).** The SOS registry regulations (2 CCR 22610.3, filed 2006) set a **$10.00**
fee for each registration. The current registration form SFL-461 says "There is no filing fee". I did not resolve
which one applies today.

**Notary cost.** Government Code 8211 caps acknowledgments at $15 per signature (FindLaw copy, current as of January 1, 2026). The National Notary
Association reports that AB 1597 (signed June 30, 2026, ch. 39) raises this to **$20 from January 1, 2027**
([NNA](https://www.nationalnotary.org/knowledge-center/news/law-updates/ca-assembly-bill-1597)). This comes from a secondary source; I did not check it on leginfo.

**Inference.** The legal steps are few. The client's real work is in steps 3, 4, 7 and 8. Steps 3 and 4 mean finding
deeds, statements and beneficiary forms, and choosing people. Steps 7 and 8 come *after* the signing ceremony, when
engagement with the firm drops off. The spec's day 6–8 "prepárese" list and the day-14 funding lesson target exactly these steps.

### F2. Who has a plan, and why people stall

**F2.1 National rates disagree with each other a lot. Choose your source deliberately.**
- **Gallup**, May 3–18, 2021, telephone, random sample of 1,016 adults: **46%** have a will. 76% of those 65+, 30% of those with
  income under $40,000, 55% of White adults and **28% of nonwhite adults** (no separate Hispanic figure). Gallup 2020: 45% have a living will.
  [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/351500/how-many-americans-have-will.aspx)
- **Caring.com with YouGov**, 2025 Wills and Estate Planning Study, 2,541 adults (2,481 in 2024), online: **24%** have a will
  (33% in 2022) and 13% a living trust. **Hispanic respondents: 14%** with a will (14.6% in 2024). 43% of people without a will
  "just haven't gotten around to it". "Nearly one in four" haven't updated their will since signing it. 1 in 10 no longer live in the
  state where they made their plan. 20% of those without a will have talked to a loved one about their wishes, and 16% have begun
  researching online. [Caring 2025](https://www.caring.com/resources/wills-survey),
  [Caring race/gender 2025](https://www.caring.com/resources/race-and-gender-estate-planning)
- **AARP Research**, April 11–16, 2024, NORC Foresight 50+ probability panel, n=1,869 adults 50+ (389 Hispanic), in English and
  Spanish: **51.2%** of all respondents have a will; **35.7% of Hispanic adults**; 29.7% of Hispanic adults say their parents had one.
  [AARP report](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/work-finances-retirement/financial-security-retirement/wealth-transfer-inheritance-wills/),
  [annotated questionnaire by race/ethnicity (PDF)](https://www.aarp.org/content/dam/aarp/research/topics/work-finances-retirement/financial-security-retirement/wealth-transfer-inheritance-wills-annotated-questionnaire-race-ethnicity.doi.10.26419-2fres.00821.001.pdf)
- *Inference about the conflict:* Caring/YouGov is an online opt-in sample of all ages. AARP is a probability panel of
  people 50+, and Gallup is a telephone probability sample. The age mix and method likely explain most of the gap. For Aprende's
  core user (Ana, 58), the **AARP Hispanic 50+ figure (about 36%)** is the best match.

**F2.2 Reasons Hispanic adults 50+ give for not having a will** (AARP 2024, people without a will; Hispanic n=250, all n=980; more than one answer allowed):

| Reason | Hispanic | All 50+ |
|---|---|---|
| Haven't gotten around to it | 54.4% | 61.4% |
| **Don't want to think about it** | **30.0%** | 16.4% |
| Don't have enough assets to make it worth it | 26.9% | 20.9% |
| Don't know how to get started / who to talk to | 19.4% | 18.6% |
| Can't afford to legally create a will | 16.1% | 14.5% |
| The process is overwhelming | 9.3% | 12.1% |
| **Don't trust the process** | **6.7%** | 1.8% |

Same survey: **64.4%** of Hispanic adults 50+ own their home. Among Hispanic adults with financial accounts (n=324),
**62.1%** named a beneficiary on all accounts, 14.0% on some and **21.4% on none**.

**F2.3 What triggers planning.** In Caring 2025, a medical diagnosis was the largest stated motivator for people without
a plan (37% in 2025; 43% in 2024). Among Hispanic respondents, "an employer benefit" as a reason for planning rose from 13% to
22% (2024→2025). "Family expansion" rose from 23% to 27%.

**F2.4 Choosing a guardian is a common stopping point.** LegalShield Parent Estate Planning Survey, July 2026, 1,036 U.S.
parents (a company that sells legal plans; not independent): 65% have not legally named a guardian. **19% "cannot decide who
the right person is or can't agree with their partner"** and 18% keep putting it off. 13% don't know how to make the choice official.
[LegalShield](https://www.legalshield.com/newsroom/the-choice-freezing-parents-two-thirds-have-no-guardian-for-their-kids)

**F2.5 Advance directives and language.** Gonzalez et al., *J. Am. Geriatrics Soc.* 2021, retrospective records of 620,948
patients 55+ in a Northern California integrated health system, 2013–2017. **20.3%** of non-Hispanic White patients and
**10.9%** of Hispanic patients had an advance directive. Among Hispanic patients, English speakers had higher odds than Spanish
speakers who needed an interpreter (aOR 2.6). Spanish speakers who did not need an interpreter had slightly higher odds (aOR 1.2).
[PubMed 33280079](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33280079/) (abstract read through Europe PMC).

**F2.6 Who the client is (AARP "Heritage & Heart", updated September 5, 2025).** Older Hispanic adults put family first:
59% name it as their top personal value. Over a quarter live in multigenerational households. 23% care for an adult, and 26% of
grandparents regularly care for a grandchild. 40% are extremely or very worried about their family's well-being after they die.
[AARP](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/aging-experience/demographics/heritage-heart-perspectives-values-hispanic-adults/)
(I did not find the survey's sample size on the page.)

**Where clients stall: a synthesis.** Rows 1–6 are evidence; rows 7–8 are partly inference.
1. *Not wanting to think about death* (AARP: 30% of Hispanic adults 50+ without a will).
2. *Choosing people*, especially a guardian (LegalShield: 19% can't decide or agree).
3. *Belief that they have too little to plan for* (AARP: 26.9% Hispanic).
4. *Not knowing how to start or whom to trust* (AARP: 19.4% and 6.7%).
5. *Cost* (AARP: 16.1% Hispanic).
6. *Gathering asset and title facts*, including deeds that still carry a deceased person's name (ACTEC Fellows).
7. *Funding and beneficiary forms after signing* (F4).
8. *Signing logistics.* The rules need two witnesses present at the same time, with exclusions. I found no data on missed
   signing appointments; this point is my inference from the requirements.

### F3. California probate: duration, fees, and the simpler routes

**F3.1 Duration.** The California Courts self-help guide says formal probate "typically takes **9 to 18 months** and can sometimes
take even longer". Administration costs "are often well over $1,000.00". The usual filing fee is **$435**. Notice must be mailed
and published.
[selfhelp: formal probate](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/probate/formal-probate). Timing floors set by statute:
- Creditors have **4 months** after letters issue, or 60 days after mailed notice, whichever is later ([9100](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=9100)).
- The inventory and appraisal is due **within 4 months** of letters ([8800](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=8800)).
- The representative must petition for final distribution or report status **within 1 year** of letters, or 18 months if a
  federal estate tax return is required ([12200](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=12200)).

Practitioner websites claim Los Angeles runs **18–24 months**. One example is livingtrust-attorneys.com, a commercial page from 2026.
I found no court data for this claim and it is not verified.

**F3.2 Statutory fees.** Probate Code 10810 (attorney) and 10800 (personal representative) use the same schedule:
4% of the first $100,000, 3% of the next $100,000, 2% of the next $800,000, 1% of the next $9 million, and 0.5% of the next $15 million.
Above $25 million the court sets a reasonable amount. The base is the inventory and appraisal value plus gains and receipts, **"without reference to encumbrances or
other obligations on estate property"** (10810(b)). These are fees for *ordinary* services; extraordinary services can add more.
[10810](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=10810), [10800](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=10800)

Worked examples (my arithmetic from the statute; the same amount goes to each of attorney and executor):

| Gross estate | Attorney | Executor | Both |
|---|---|---|---|
| $300,000 | $9,000 | $9,000 | $18,000 |
| $500,000 | $13,000 | $13,000 | $26,000 |
| $750,000 | $18,000 | $18,000 | $36,000 |
| $1,000,000 | $23,000 | $23,000 | $46,000 |

*Inference:* A house worth $750,000 with a $500,000 mortgage still produces fees on $750,000. Many LA homeowners do not expect this.

**F3.3 Simpler routes (amounts for deaths on or after April 1, 2025; next adjustment April 1, 2028).** Source: Judicial Council form
**DE-300, rev. April 28, 2025** ([PDF](https://courts.ca.gov/sites/default/files/courts/default/2024-11/de300.pdf)) and
[selfhelp: simple transfer](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/probate/simple-transfer). The amounts are adjusted every 3 years by CPI
([890](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=890)).

| Route | Statute | Death 4/1/2022–3/31/2025 | Death on/after 4/1/2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small estate affidavit (personal property; 40 days after death; no court) | 13100–13101 | $184,500 | **$208,850** |
| Petition for the **primary residence** | 13151–13154 | $184,500 (any real property) | **$750,000** (main home in California only) |
| Affidavit for real property of small value | 13200 | $61,500 | $69,625 |
| Wages excluded from the estate value | 13050(c) | $18,450 | $20,875 |
| Spouse's collection of unpaid wages | 13600–13601 | $18,450 | $20,875 |
| Small estate set-aside for the spouse and minor children | 6602, 6609 | $95,325 | $107,900 |

- Statute text (leginfo): 13100 still prints the base amount "$166,250, as adjusted periodically in accordance with Section 890".
  Since AB 2016 (Stats. 2024, ch. 331, effective January 1, 2025) it excludes "any property included in a petition filed under Section 13151".
  13151 allows the petition when the decedent's "primary residence in this state" has a gross value of no more than "$750,000" and 40 days have passed.
  The petitioner must notify each heir and devisee within 5 business days. [13100](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=13100), [13151](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=13151)
- **Conflict:** the self-help page "Small estate affidavit to transfer personal property" still says the estate must be "$184,500 or less"
  for deaths on or after April 1, 2022 ([selfhelp: small estate](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/probate/small-estate), read 2026-11-18).
  DE-300 and the self-help "simple transfer" page say $208,850. The page is out of date. Trust DE-300.
- *Inference:* The 13151 petition is still a court case, with filing, notice and a hearing. It is not "no court at all". Because 13100 excludes
  property in a 13151 petition, a family with a main home worth $750,000 or less **and** other California property worth $208,850 or
  less may avoid full probate entirely without a trust. This weakens a blanket "you need a trust to avoid probate" message for
  some Aprende clients. It also matters for the firm's advice on trust versus will (an attorney question, not a lesson claim).

**F3.4 Spouses.** If a spouse dies without a will and property passes to the surviving spouse under 6401, or a will leaves it to the
surviving spouse, "no administration is necessary" (13500). A spousal or domestic partner property petition (13650; Judicial Council form
DE-221) asks the court for an order confirming this, and can also confirm the survivor's own half of community property.
[13500](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=13500), [13650](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=13650), [selfhelp DE-221](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/jcc-form/DE-221)

**F3.5 Revocable transfer on death (TOD) deed.**
- Requirements: the transferor signs and dates the deed. Two witnesses "present at the same time" sign it. It is acknowledged before a
  notary (5624, as amended by SB 315, effective January 1, 2022). It must be recorded within 60 days of notarization (5626).
  [5624](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=5624), [5626](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=5626)
- **Sunset:** the TOD deed law "shall remain in effect only until January 1, 2032". Deeds signed before then remain valid (5600(c)). [5600](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=5600)
- Sacramento County Public Law Library: each owner signs their own deed, so a married couple records two. The property description
  must match the current deed exactly. At death the beneficiaries must notify heirs, record an affidavit of death, and notify Medi-Cal if
  the owner received benefits. [SacLaw](https://saclaw.org/resource_library/californias-transfer-on-death-deed-one-option-to-avoid-probate-on-your-home/)
- California Law Revision Commission, *Revocable Transfer on Death Deed: Follow-Up Study* (November 2019): it heard "anecdotal reports
  that some California title insurers will not issue policies" during the 3-year period when the property could be pulled back into the
  estate. It "did not find evidence that an RTODD is any more prone to financial abuse than any other kind of instrument", but
  recommended witnessing as a safeguard. That recommendation became law in 2022.
  [CLRC](https://clrc.ca.gov/pub/Printed-Reports/Pub241-L3032.1.pdf)

### F4. Common execution failures and their consequences

| Failure | What the law says happens | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Will not properly witnessed** | Without two qualifying witnesses the will fails, unless the proponent proves "by clear and convincing evidence" that the testator intended it to be the will (the harmless-error rule, 6110(c)(2), added by Stats. 2008, ch. 53, effective 2009). This means a court fight with a high burden of proof. The State Bar's Trusts & Estates Section proposal that led to it **[older, 2008]** calls the earlier law "unintuitive" and cites *Estate of Saueressig* (2006): witnesses must sign during the testator's lifetime. | [6110](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=6110); [State Bar T&E-2008-04](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/portals/0/documents/legislation/T&E-2008-04.pdf) |
| **Beneficiary acts as witness** | The gift to that witness is presumed to come from duress, menace, fraud or undue influence. If the presumption is not rebutted, the witness takes no more than their intestate share (6112(c)–(d)). | [6112](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=6112) |
| **Handwritten (holographic) will** | Valid "whether or not witnessed, if the signature and the material provisions are in the handwriting of the testator" (6111(a)). If it is **undated**, it is invalid to the extent it conflicts with another will whose order is in doubt, or if the testator lacked capacity at any time when it might have been written (6111(b)). Practitioner firms describe holographic wills as a frequent source of contests (for example, Adell Law; Trust Law Partners). I found **no data on how often this happens**. | [6111](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=6111) |
| **Trust signed but not funded** | Property left outside the trust passes under the pour-over will, which may require probate, or needs a Heggstad petition (Probate Code 850). *Estate of Heggstad* (1993) 16 Cal.App.4th 943 **[older]** held that a written declaration that the settlor holds property as trustee can be enough. The State Bar T&E Section (2015 proposal) **[older]**: "There are no clear guidelines… the result of a Heggstad petition can be very unpredictable", leading to "greater attorney time – and therefore larger bills". | [Heggstad (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/16/943.html); [State Bar T&E-2015-10](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/sites/default/files/portals/0/documents/legislation/2015/TE-2015-10-trustee_property_petition.pdf) |
| **Beneficiary designation not updated** | After divorce, revocable nonprobate transfers to the ex-spouse fail (5040), and so do will gifts (6122). **But** for employer plans governed by ERISA (401(k), employer life insurance), the U.S. Supreme Court held in *Egelhoff v. Egelhoff*, 532 U.S. 141 (2001) **[older]**, that federal law preempts state revocation-on-divorce statutes. The ex-spouse named on the form can collect. AARP 2024: 21.4% of Hispanic adults 50+ with accounts named no beneficiary at all. | [5040](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=5040), [6122](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=6122); [Egelhoff (Justia)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/532/141/) — I read Egelhoff's holding from Justia/Cornell search summaries; the page itself was blocked |
| **Joint tenancy with children instead of a plan** | SacLaw: adding heirs to title makes them full owners immediately. Risks include the children's creditors, refinancing problems, loss of Prop 13 protection, mortgage acceleration and gift tax filing. | [SacLaw](https://saclaw.org/resource_library/californias-transfer-on-death-deed-one-option-to-avoid-probate-on-your-home/) |
| **No plan, house passes to many heirs ("heirs' property")** | Housing Assistance Council with UnidosUS, 2026: in Latino households, informal inheritance has led to homes "passed down without clear title", which "can restrict access to loans, repairs, disaster recovery assistance". The Boston College Center for Retirement Research (blog, 2023) reports that intestacy can split a house among multiple and unintended heirs. | [HAC/UnidosUS](https://ruralhome.org/heirs-property-in-latino-communities/); [CRR](https://crr.bc.edu/blacks-and-hispanics-often-lack-wills-but-need-them/) |
| **POA refused by a bank** | A third party that unreasonably refuses a properly executed *statutory form* POA can be compelled to honor it and must pay attorney's fees (4406). For other POAs, the agent can give an affidavit; an unreasonable refusal then exposes the third party to fees (4305–4306). | [4406](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=4406), [4306](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=4306) |
| **AHCD witnessed by the wrong person** | The patient's provider or its employees, care-facility operators or employees, and the named agent cannot witness. If two relatives or heirs witness, the 4674(e) rule is not met: at least one witness must be unrelated and not entitled to inherit (4674). In a skilled nursing facility the directive "is not effective unless" an ombudsman or patient advocate witnesses it (4675). | [4674](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=4674), [4675](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=4675) |
| **Original will lost or locked in a box** | The custodian must deliver it to the court within 30 days and is "liable for all damages" for failing to (8200). Opening a sole-name safe deposit box needs the key, a death certificate and ID (331). | [8200](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=8200) |

### F5. Notario fraud and other bad actors

- **Law.** Government Code 8219.5(c): "Literal translation of the phrase 'notary public' into Spanish, hereby defined as 'notario publico'
  or 'notario,' is prohibited." Non-attorney notaries who advertise in another language must post "I am not an attorney and, therefore,
  cannot give legal advice about immigration or any other legal matters." Violations bring suspension, and a second offense brings permanent revocation.
  [FindLaw copy](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-8219-5/)
- **State Bar** ([immigration consultants page](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/public/concerns-about-attorney/avoid-legal-services-fraud/avoid-fraud-immigration-consultants)):
  "In some Latinx communities, immigration consultants and other nonattorneys advertise their services as notarios or notarios públicos… in some
  countries, notarios have training similar to lawyers." The State Bar offers a multilingual hotline (800-843-9053) and a "Have You Been a Victim of Nonattorney or
  Notario Fraud?" pamphlet in Spanish.
- **Complaint data.** State Bar 2025 Annual Discipline Report, Table SR-9, "UPL by Nonattorneys". Cases opened: FY2020 761, FY2021 680,
  FY2022 660, FY2023 845, FY2024 908, **FY2025 1,223**. There is no breakdown by practice area or by "notario".
  [PDF](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/2025-Annual-Discipline-Report.pdf)
- **LA County DCBA:** immigration consultants must post a $100,000 bond with the Secretary of State, give typed receipts, and "cannot… give you legal
  advice… keep your original documents… charge you upfront". [DCBA](https://dcba.lacounty.gov/portfolio/immigration-services-fraud/)
- **Public Counsel (LA legal aid):** "In some Latin American countries, 'notarios públicos' have similar educational training and professional
  duties as lawyers". The page includes LA victim accounts with losses of $80,000 and over $100,000.
  [Public Counsel](https://publiccounsel.org/issues/immigrants-rights/notario-fraud/)
- **Estate-planning-specific bad actors.** The California Attorney General's "Living Trusts Scams" page: "living trust mills" sell
  "estate planning that turns out to be defective" plus annuities. They solicit seniors "at churches, assisted living centers", with sellers who "pose as
  attorneys or paralegals". It also notes "Not everyone needs a living trust" and "Always ask for a copy of any documents you sign… make sure
  you have a copy of the final executed documents". [CA AG](https://oag.ca.gov/consumers/general/living_trust_scams)
- **Legal Document Assistants** are a lawful alternative but cannot give advice. SacLaw: they "Under no circumstances can… provide legal advice or opinion"
  (B&P 6400 et seq.). [SacLaw](https://saclaw.org/resource_library/legal-document-assistants/)
- **Gap:** I found **no** FTC, State Bar or county data that counts notario fraud involving *wills or trusts*. The FTC's 2021 "Serving
  Communities of Color" report exists, but its URL returned 404 on 2026-11-18 and I did not read it. Any statement that notarios commonly
  sell wills in LA would be inference, not evidence.

### F6. Costs from outside probate that families do not expect

- **Proposition 19 (operative February 16, 2021).** For a parent-to-child transfer of the family home, the BOE fact sheet says "The transferee
  must live in the home as their primary residence within one year of transfer". The child must file for the homeowners' exemption within one year.
  The excluded value is capped at the taxable value plus $1 million, adjusted for inflation. "In cases where the transferor died, the date of death is
  considered the date of transfer." [BOE pub 801](https://www.boe.ca.gov/pdf/pub801.pdf)
- **Medi-Cal estate recovery.** DHCS: for members who died on or after January 1, 2017, "Repayment will be limited only to estate assets subject to
  probate". Notice of death must be given to DHCS within 90 days. [DHCS](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/estate-recovery-program/)
- *Inference:* For a Boyle Heights homeowner on Medi-Cal, putting the house in a funded trust or a TOD deed may keep it out of estate recovery.
  This is a strong, concrete reason to finish funding. It is also squarely individual advice, so the app should raise it as a question for the attorney.

## Implications for Aprende

Each implication names the app part, the finding it rests on, and a confidence level. "Confidence" is how sure I am that the change helps,
not how sure I am of the law.

### Lesson content
1. **Add the 2025 amounts to the probate bonus lesson, keyed to the date of death:** $208,850 personal property affidavit and the
   $750,000 primary-residence petition, plus the "40 days after death" rule. Cite DE-300 and 13100/13151, **not** the out-of-date self-help
   small-estate page. Rests on F3.3. **High.**
2. **Add duration and a fee example to the probate lesson:** "9 to 18 months" (self-help) and the 10810 schedule, with one worked example
   ("on a $500,000 estate, $13,000 for the attorney and $13,000 for the executor; the mortgage is not subtracted").
   The trust lesson already says "at least 9 months"; align it to the court's "9 to 18 months". Rests on F3.1–F3.2. **High.**
3. **Nuance the "trust avoids probate" message.** Since April 2025, a main home worth $750,000 or less can pass by a simpler court petition.
   State it as general information and route "do I need a trust?" to the attorney. Rests on F3.3. **Medium.** The legal point is certain;
   how the firm wants to frame it is a firm decision.
4. **Change the holographic-will items in `will.yaml`.** Keep the 6111 rule, but add that an undated handwritten will can fail (6111(b))
   and that a will signed by the firm with witnesses avoids these disputes. Also remove any impression that a handwritten will is an equal alternative.
   Rests on F4. **High.**
5. **Add "a witness should not be someone who inherits" to the will lesson**, not only to the signing guide (6112). Rests on F4. **High.**
6. **Beneficiary designations: add the ERISA warning** to the beneficiarios bonus lesson. The lesson cites 5040; add that for employer 401(k)s
   and employer life insurance, federal law can let an ex-spouse named on the form collect anyway (*Egelhoff*), so the form itself must be changed.
   Rests on F4. **High.**
7. **The TOD deed is not in the lessons.** Consider one item in the probate or community bonus lesson. Cover what it is, its requirements
   (two witnesses at the same time plus a notary, recorded within 60 days), that each spouse records their own, and the January 1, 2032 sunset.
   Mention it as general information only. The firm's core scope is will/trust/POA/AHCD; whether to mention TOD deeds is a firm decision.
   Rests on F3.5. **Medium.**
8. **Prop 19 and Medi-Cal estate recovery as "things families don't expect" bonus items.** State the general rule and send "what does this mean
   for me?" to the attorney. Rests on F6. **Medium.**

### Intake questions ("Tus datos")
9. **Ask how the house is titled, and who is on the deed now**, including whether a deceased relative is still on it. Ask for a photo or
   copy of the deed. ACTEC Fellows name this as a common funding problem. Rests on F1 row 7, F4 (heirs' property). **High.**
10. **Ask about every account with a beneficiary form, and who is named on it**, flagging an ex-spouse and employer plans. Rests on F2.2
    (21.4% none) and F4. **High.**
11. **Ask whether the client receives or expects Medi-Cal**, and whether any child plans to live in the home. Ask these as facts for the attorney,
    with no advice in the app. Rests on F6. **Medium.**
12. **Ask whether the client lives in or may move to a skilled nursing facility**, because the AHCD then needs an ombudsman witness. Rests on F4. **High.**
13. **For guardians, let the client name a first choice and an alternate, and allow "not decided yet"** without blocking progress.
    Let the attorney see "undecided" as a flag. Rests on F2.4. **Medium.** The survey is from a commercial seller.
14. **Ask where any existing will, trust or handwritten note is**, and whether there is an old will. Old and new documents together
    are where 6111(b) disputes arise. Rests on F4. **Medium.**

### Character tone
15. **El Oso's calm mode should open the will lesson too, not only the health directive.** For Hispanic adults 50+ without a will,
    "don't want to think about it" is twice as common as for all respondents (30.0% vs 16.4%). Frame the first will screen around protecting
    family ("para que su familia no tenga problemas con la casa"), not around death. Rests on F2.2, F2.6. **Medium.** The evidence is about
    reasons for not having a will, not about tested messages.
16. **Answer "I don't have enough to need a plan" directly.** Use a scripted reply built on the house (64.4% of Hispanic adults 50+ own one)
    and intestacy splitting it among heirs. Rests on F2.2, F4. **Medium.**
17. **Language:** AD completion is lowest among Hispanic patients who need an interpreter (F2.5). Full Spanish audio, with no switch to English,
    is a strength. Keep the AHCD lesson Spanish-first and slow. **Medium.** The study measured a health system, not an app.

### Timeline and reminders
18. **The riskiest period is after day 13. Plan reminders past day 14:** funding check-ins at 2, 6 and 12 weeks until staff mark `funding_done`,
    then an annual review and a "life event" prompt. These follow ACTEC's "every five years, sooner if…" and the triggers in Caring 2025
    (property, birth, marriage, diagnosis). Rests on F1 rows 7–9, F4 (unfunded trusts). **High** for the funding reminders;
    **medium** for the exact intervals (my choice).
19. **Day 11–12 signing guide:** add an explicit check of who the witnesses will be. Do any of them inherit (6112)? Are any the agent (4122/4674)?
    Are any the client's health care provider or its employee (4674)? For the AHCD, is at least one witness unrelated and not an heir (4674(e))? A son and daughter-in-law witnessing their mother's directive would fail this. Collect the witnesses' names before the day, if the firm wants to. Rests on F1 row 6. **High.**

### Pricing and discounts
20. **Show what the client saves, using the court's numbers.** A side-by-side such as "$3,000 plan vs. statutory probate fees of $26,000 on a
    $500,000 estate" is true and persuasive. It must carry the caveats: this is an example, the client's estate may qualify for a simpler route,
    and the attorney advises. Rests on F3.2–F3.3. **Medium.** The comparison is accurate but could read as advice or a promise; the firm must approve the wording.
21. **Put the notary cost in the price breakdown**, if the firm passes it on: $15 per signature today, reportedly $20 from January 1, 2027 (AB 1597;
    check on leginfo before use). Rests on F1. **Low.** This depends on firm practice.
22. **Consider a discount or seal for *finishing funding*** (staff-confirmed), not only for lessons, because funding is the most common failure point.
    Rests on F4. **Medium.** There is no data on incentive effects; it is consistent with the app's existing incentive design.

### Trust and safety copy
23. **Answer the notario confusion openly, in Spanish:** "En California, un notario público no es abogado. El abogado Pankaj Raval tiene licencia de la
    State Bar of California: verifíquelo aquí [link to the State Bar's attorney search]." Rests on F5 (8219.5; State Bar page; 6.7% of Hispanic
    respondents "don't trust the process"). **High.**
24. **Use the Attorney General's own advice as app copy:** "Pida copia de todo lo que firme", "No firme bajo presión", "No todos necesitan un fideicomiso".
    Saying this ourselves separates the firm from living trust mills. Rests on F5. **High.**
25. **Never pair the trust with annuities or other financial products** in app copy or character suggestions (AG warning). Rests on F5. **High.**
26. **Do not call the POA "poder notarial" without explaining it.** It may be the term clients search for. But California allows *either* a notary or two
    witnesses (4121), and the word "notarial" feeds the notario confusion. *Inference:* use "poder legal para finanzas (poder notarial)" on first use.
    **Low–medium.** This needs the native-speaker review (Q-L1).

### Firm workflow
27. **Make funding a staff-tracked checklist item per asset, with evidence** (recorded deed, bank confirmation), and close the matter only when
    it is complete. Rests on F4 (Heggstad unpredictability). **High.**
28. **Make the firm the custodian of originals, or record where they are kept**, and record who the executor and successor trustee are told to contact.
    The 30-day lodging duty (8200) applies to whoever holds the will. Rests on F1 row 8. **Medium.** This is a firm policy choice.
29. **Screen for clients in skilled nursing facilities and arrange an ombudsman witness.** For an **electronic** AHCD, a notary is mandatory (4673(b)).
    Rests on F4. **High.**
30. **Witness logistics: have two disinterested staff witnesses available at every signing.** Staff are not the client's health care provider, so they
    can witness the AHCD. Have a notary available for the POA and AHCD if the firm prefers notarization. Rests on F1 row 6. **High.**
31. **Content maintenance: add DE-300 to `sources.yaml`** with a recheck date of April 1, 2028, when the amounts next change. Rests on F3.3. **High.**
