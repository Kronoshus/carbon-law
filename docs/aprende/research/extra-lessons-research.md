## (a) Planning for minor children

**Guardian of the person and guardian of the estate.** "Guardianship of the person": the guardian makes legal decisions
for the child and is responsible for care; "guardianship of the estate": someone manages the child's finances, needed "if a
child has a lot of money, income, or property", with "very strict rules" (California Courts self-help,
[Probate guardianships in California](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/guardianship)).

**Who may nominate.** "A parent may nominate a guardian of the person or estate, or both, of a minor child" where the other
parent nominates or consents to the same guardian, or the other parent is dead, lacks capacity, or would not need to consent
to an adoption ([PC 1500](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=1500)). Anyone (a parent or "any other person") may nominate a guardian for
property the minor receives from them, by gift, deed, trust, will, insurance or benefits ([PC 1501](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=1501)).

**How and when.** A nomination may be made in the petition, at the hearing, "or in a writing signed either before or after
the petition"; the court "shall give due weight to the nomination of a guardian of the person" under Family Code 3043. A
writing may make the nomination effective only on conditions "including ... the subsequent absence, legal incapacity, or
death" of the parent, and it stays effective despite later absence, incapacity or death unless it says otherwise
([PC 1502](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=1502), amended by Stats. 2025, ch. 664 (AB 495), effective 2026-01-01).

**What the court does.** The court "may appoint a guardian of the person or estate ... or both"; for the person, it follows
the Family Code custody chapters (best interest, parental preference); for the estate it "shall appoint" a PC 1500 nominee
unless unsuitable, and a PC 1501 nominee for the covered property ([PC 1514](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=1514)).

**If no one was nominated.** An adult (often a relative) files to become guardian, the family is served, a court
investigator reports, and the judge decides; relatives can object at the hearing (self-help
[guardianship](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/guardianship)). A nomination does not bind the judge for the person, but it is
the parent's written wish and gets "due weight" (PC 1502(a)).

**Short absences without court.** Caregiver's Authorization Affidavit: no court filing, no notary; any adult caregiver can
enroll the child in school and consent to school-related medical care; a relative can also consent to other medical care;
parents can cancel it; it does not give legal custody (self-help
[Options other than a guardianship](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/guardianship/other-options);
[Fam. Code 6550](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=FAM&sectionNum=6550), amended by AB 495). The same page describes a parent-signed written child-care
agreement (a power of attorney for child care), which keeps legal custody with the parent.

**Joint guardianship.** PC 2105(f): a parent with a terminal condition may be appointed joint guardian with a nominee. New
PC 2105(g) (AB 495, effective 2026-01-01): if a custodial parent is "temporarily unavailable due to circumstances" specified
in the nomination (the statute lists examples such as a serious medical condition, disability or military service), the
court may appoint the parent and the nominee as joint guardians; not over a noncustodial parent's objection without a
detriment finding; a presumption favors ending it when the parent is available again; the records are confidential
([PC 2105](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=2105)). *The lesson names only medical and military examples, under the app's no-immigration
rule.*

**Money for minors (CUTMA and trusts).** A custodian transfers the property to the minor at 18 unless delayed
([PC 3920](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=3920)); a will or trust may continue the custodianship to a specified age "not later than
... 25" ([PC 3920.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=3920.5)(c)); a lifetime gift may delay only to 21 (3920.5(e)). The California
Statutory Will says a custodian can manage assets for children "until they reach any age from 18 to 25"
([PC 6240](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=6240)). A trust can hold property longer and set its own terms; that choice is for the
attorney.

## (b) Things families don't expect (one line each)

**Transfer-on-death (TOD) deed.** A revocable TOD deed "makes a donative transfer of real property to a named beneficiary",
"operates on the transferor's death" and "remains revocable" ([PC 5614](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=5614)); it covers 1–4 unit
homes and condominium units, not farmland over 40 acres ([PC 5610](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=5610)). It is not effective unless
signed, dated, witnessed by two witnesses present at the same time, and notarized ([PC 5624](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=5624)), and
recorded within 60 days of notarization ([PC 5626](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=5626)). **Sunset:** the part "shall remain in effect
only until January 1, 2032" unless extended; deeds executed before then remain valid ([PC 5600](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=5600)(c),
as amended by SB 315, Stats. 2021, ch. 215). No later extension found as of 2026-11-22.

**Prop 19 (property tax on an inherited home).** Since 2021-02-16 the parent-child exclusion applies only to a principal
residence that becomes the child's principal residence, with the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption filed within a
year ([R&T Code 63.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=RTC&sectionNum=63.2)(a)(1)). The excluded value is the taxable value plus $1 million, adjusted
every two years: $1,044,586 for transfers from 2025-02-16 through 2027-02-15
([BOE news release NR 25-02](https://www.boe.ca.gov/news/2025/nr-25-02.htm); [BOE Proposition 19](https://www.boe.ca.gov/prop19/)).
Other property (rentals, second homes) no longer qualifies.

**Medi-Cal estate recovery.** For members who died on or after 2017-01-01, "repayment will be limited only to estate assets
subject to probate", and to nursing-facility and home- and community-based services and related hospital and drug costs
([DHCS Estate Recovery Program](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/estate-recovery-program/)). The claim applies to members 55
or older when served (or in a nursing facility), and not when a spouse or registered domestic partner, a child under 21, or a
blind or disabled child survives; DHCS must consider hardship waivers, including a "homestead of modest value"
([W&I Code 14009.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=WIC&sectionNum=14009.5), SB 833). *Context, not in the lesson:* the Medi-Cal asset limit returned on
2026-01-01 ($130,000 for one person) and the 2026-27 budget lowers it to $21,000 on 2027-07-01 (California Health Advocates,
[budget update](https://cahealthadvocates.org/state-budget-update-many-of-governors-cuts-rejected-but-medi-cals-asset-limit-significantly-reduced/));
the attorney may want this for intake. Federal H.R. 1 (2025) adds a $1 million home-equity cap for long-term-care Medicaid from
2028-10-01 (Disability Rights California); eligibility again, not recovery.
